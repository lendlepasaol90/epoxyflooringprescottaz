Commercial epoxy flooring for Prescott businesses

Prescott’s businesses range from restaurants and saloons around the Courthouse Square to galleries, boutiques, clinics and offices across town. We provide seamless, durable floors and plan each project around your trading hours.

A durable floor for hospitality

Commercial floors in Prescott work in some unusual settings. Many downtown businesses occupy buildings more than a century old, with concrete that has been patched, extended and covered several times. Others are in modern shopping centers or office parks. What they share is a need for a floor that is easy to clean, resistant to spills and wear, and installed without closing for long.

Resin flooring meets those needs well. This page explains how we approach commercial projects, the systems suited to different businesses, and how we work in older buildings and occupied premises.

Working in historic downtown buildings

Older commercial buildings present particular challenges. Floors may combine concrete of several ages, old tile, adhesive residues and uneven areas where repairs were made long ago. Below-grade spaces may have moisture to manage. Neighbors share walls and sometimes ventilation, and access may be limited to a front door on a busy street.

We plan for all of this before work begins. Moisture is tested; old flooring and adhesives are removed; uneven areas are ground or leveled; and dust is controlled with vacuum-attached equipment and containment. Work is scheduled around your trading hours and, where needed, your neighbors'. The aim is a sound, attractive floor without disturbance to the building or the street.

Galleries and retail

Prescott's galleries and boutiques need floors that support the work on display without competing with it. Satin solid colors in soft greys or warm neutrals reflect light gently and keep attention on the art. A metallic finish in a restrained palette can lend a reception area or showroom some character. Topcoats are chosen to resist the scuffs of constant foot traffic and the occasional dragged display stand, and the floor can be cleaned quickly before opening.

Keeping your doors open

Closure is often the most costly part of a floor project. We plan to minimize it.

Out-of-hours work. Smaller premises can frequently be completed between closing and opening, or across a weekend, using rapid-curing systems.

Staged installation. Larger premises are divided into zones so that part of the business can continue operating.

Containment. Vacuum-attached grinders, temporary barriers and low-odor products protect stock, staff and neighboring tenants.

A written schedule is provided before work begins, showing when each area will be unavailable.

Seasonal trade and winter floors

Prescott's visitor trade peaks around summer weekends and the many events held in and around the square, while winter brings snow and slush to every entrance. Entries take the most wear, so we plan them carefully: additional texture, recesses or space for walk-off mats and topcoats with high abrasion resistance.

Many businesses choose to schedule floor work in quieter months, often late winter or early spring, when trading is lighter and there is more flexibility in the calendar.

The importance of the installer

A failed commercial floor brings more than an untidy appearance; it may create a slip hazard, complicate an inspection or force a second closure. Moisture testing, a system matched to your chemicals, careful detailing at drains and walls, and appropriate texture prevent those problems. We take the time to get them right.

Restaurants and food service

Commercial kitchens subject floors to hot water, grease, acids and constant mopping. Urethane cement systems resist thermal shock and food-related chemicals far better than ordinary coatings, and can be carried up walls as coves and into drains so water cannot penetrate. Dining rooms and bars may then receive a more decorative finish with a hard-wearing topcoat.

Pricing and maintenance

Price depends on the existing flooring, the condition of the concrete, the system specified, details such as coves and drains, and the schedule. Out-of-hours or staged work requires more planning. Everything is itemized so the trade-offs are clear.

Maintenance consists of routine cleaning with suitable products, mats at entrances and periodic renewal of the topcoat in the busiest areas. Written guidance is provided for your staff.

Medical and dental practices

Prescott has a large and growing number of medical, dental and therapy practices serving the region. Their floors need to be easy to disinfect, comfortable for staff who stand all day and quiet under rolling equipment. A seamless resin floor with integral coved edges leaves no joints for contaminants to collect in, and the topcoat can be chosen to resist the disinfectants the practice uses. Soft, calm colors and a satin sheen reduce glare under clinical lighting. Work can be staged over weekends so appointments continue.

Salons and spas

Hair dyes, chemical treatments, water and constant sweeping make salon floors hard to keep looking fresh. A chemical-resistant topcoat over a solid or flake base prevents staining and cleans up quickly between clients. Light colors keep the space bright; darker tones hide hair and product between sweeps. We can also coat back rooms and wash areas with additional texture for safety.

Event venues and halls

Event spaces, halls and venues see enormous variety: dancing, catering, rolling tables, spilled drinks and frequent resets. A durable, seamless floor with a satin or textured finish handles all of it and is easily cleaned after each event. For venues with a period character, warm neutral colors keep the floor in harmony with the building.

Breweries and tasting rooms

Prescott's breweries, distilleries and tasting rooms combine two very different floors under one roof. Production areas face hot caustic washdown, sugary spills, acids and standing water, and need a urethane cement system with coved edges and careful drain detailing. Front-of-house areas face crowds, dropped glasses and stools being dragged, and can take a more decorative resin floor with a durable topcoat. We plan the transition between the two so it is both watertight and attractive.

Questions about commercial floors

Can you work in a building on the historic square?

Yes. We plan access, dust control and timing carefully in older buildings, and coordinate with owners and neighbors where necessary.

Can the work be done outside trading hours?

Often. Smaller premises can be completed overnight or over a weekend using rapid-curing systems.

What floor suits a restaurant kitchen?

Usually urethane cement, which tolerates hot water, grease and constant cleaning, with texture for safe footing.

Will the floor be slippery?

Not where it matters. Kitchens, bar runs and doorways receive a coarser grit; dining and display areas a finer one that is easier to mop.

Do you remove old flooring?

Yes. Old tile, vinyl and adhesive are removed so the system bonds directly to the concrete.

How long will the floor last?

With an appropriate system and routine care, many years. High-traffic areas can be refreshed with a new topcoat rather than replaced.

Can you match our brand colors?

Solid systems can be tinted closely to most colors, and samples are tested on site first.

Can you coat a commercial kitchen with existing floor drains?

Yes. Drains are cleaned and detailed, and the coating is carried into them so water cannot work its way beneath the floor.

Can production and taproom areas have different floors?

Yes. We frequently install a heavy-duty system in production and a decorative finish in the taproom, joined by a clean, sealed transition.

Do you provide documentation for landlords or building owners?

Yes. We supply product data sheets, safety information and a schedule to support any approval the building requires.
