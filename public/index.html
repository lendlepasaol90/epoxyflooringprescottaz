Epoxy flooring with an eye for the details

A good floor is mostly invisible work: the grinding, the patching, the testing and the patience to wait for the right conditions.

Prescott is a town that values craftsmanship. People restore Victorian porches, keep vintage cars in their garages and take pride in homes that have been cared for over decades. We approach concrete floors the same way.

Before anything is applied, we look carefully at the slab. Older homes may have paint, sealers or patched cracks from years past. Newer homes may carry a builder's curing compound. Garages near the national forest often show winter damage near the door. All of it is ground away or repaired so the coating bonds to sound concrete, which is what decides whether a floor lasts three years or fifteen.

Then we recommend a system to suit the space: a garage floor coating with a decorative flake finish, a seamless interior floor for a basement or casita, a fast-curing polyaspartic or flexible polyurea build, a metallic or decorative flake finish, a protective patio coating, or a durable commercial or hangar and warehouse floor. Every estimate is free and provided in writing.

Flake finish, slip-resistant topcoat

Garage floor coatings for every era of home

Garages around Prescott range from detached carriage houses behind older homes to three-car garages in Prescott Lakes and the Ranch. They share a set of challenges: snow and slush tracked in through winter, road deicer, spring dust, strong sun at altitude and the oil and fluids that come with vehicles and equipment.

Our garage systems begin with diamond grinding to remove whatever is on the slab and open the concrete. Cracks, spalls and winter scaling are repaired. Then we apply a bonding base coat, a broadcast of decorative flakes and a UV-stable, slip-resistant topcoat that will not yellow by the door.

  • Old paint, sealers and failed coatings removed completely
  • Repairs for cracks and winter flaking included in the estimate
  • Finishes chosen to suit the home, from classic to contemporary

Residential epoxy flooring inside the home

Many Prescott homes have rooms on concrete that work harder than any other part of the house: basements in older homes, laundry and mudrooms, casitas, workshops and studios. A seamless coated floor in those spaces is easy to clean, stands up to wet boots and dogs, and can look as refined as the rest of the home.

Interior work calls for more care than a garage. Finishes are chosen to complement cabinetry, trim and wall color, sheen is matched to the light in the room and moisture is tested before anything is applied, which matters especially in basements and older slabs. Questions worth asking any flooring contractor

Whoever you choose to coat your floor, a few questions will tell you a great deal about the quality of the work to come. A good contractor will answer each of them clearly and put the answers in writing.

  • How will the slab be prepared? Diamond grinding is the standard for a lasting bond; acid etching or a quick wash is not.
  • Will you test for moisture? Especially important for basements, older slabs and interior rooms.
  • How are cracks and joints treated? Rigid, flexible or left alone, and why.
  • What topcoat will be used, and is it light-stable? At this elevation, it matters.
  • What are the cure times at this time of year? A good answer depends on temperature.
  • What does the written estimate include? Every layer and repair should be listed.

Planning

My home is older. Is the garage slab still a good candidate?

Usually, yes. Older slabs often carry stains, paint, patched cracks and some surface wear, but once they are ground and repaired they take a coating very well. We examine the concrete at the estimate and tell you plainly how much repair it needs.

What is the best season for a coating project in Prescott?

May and early June, and again from late September into October, are the easiest months. High summer works well if we begin early, though porches and walks are planned around the afternoon storms. In the colder months we concentrate on heated garages and interiors, bringing in temporary heat where a space needs it.

Do you provide written estimates?

Always. Each estimate lists the preparation, repairs, every layer of the system and the expected cure times, so you can compare options and other quotes on equal terms.

The finished floor

How long until I can use my garage again?

Most garages accept foot traffic within a day and vehicles within one to three days, depending on the system and temperature. Fast-curing polyaspartic topcoats shorten that window considerably.

Will snowmelt make the floor slippery?

We add a fine slip-resistant aggregate to garage and entry topcoats, and flake finishes carry natural texture. Pushing meltwater out with a floor squeegee after storms keeps the surface safe and clean.

How should I clean a coated floor?

A dust mop or soft broom for routine cleaning, and warm water with a neutral cleaner when it needs more. Avoid harsh solvents and citrus degreasers, and rinse away deicer residue a few times each winter.

Businesses and hangars

Do you coat aircraft hangar floors?

Yes. Hangar floors need coatings that resist fuel, hydraulic fluid and tire marks, reflect light for inspection work and stand up to tugs and jacks. We discuss the specific aircraft and maintenance activity before recommending a system.

Can you work in older downtown buildings?

We do. Older commercial buildings often have patched or uneven concrete and tenants on every side, so we plan carefully for preparation, dust control and access.
