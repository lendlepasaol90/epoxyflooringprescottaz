Metallic epoxy floors with depth and character

A metallic floor is closer to a work of craft than a coating. Pigments are guided by hand through clear resin and settle into patterns that no other floor will share.

Request an estimate(928) 555-0186

A seamless floor in a living space

Prescott has a long tradition of craftsmanship, from the brickwork around the Courthouse Square to the restored woodwork in its Victorian homes and the art in its downtown galleries. Metallic epoxy fits comfortably within that tradition. Each floor is made by hand, in place, and the result depends as much on skill as on materials.

The effect is a surface with real depth, as though you were looking into polished stone. As light moves through a room over the course of a day, the floor changes with it. This page explains how metallic floors are made, where they are most successful and what to consider before choosing one.

The making of a metallic floor

Metallic pigments are fine particles of mica coated with metallic oxides. Suspended in clear epoxy, they reflect light from within the resin. After the material is poured, the installer works it with rollers, trowels and brushes, guiding the pigment into veins, drifts or soft clouds.

The floor continues to change for a short time after that. As the resin levels itself, the pigments shift slightly before setting. That final, natural movement gives metallic floors their depth and ensures no two are alike.

A tinted base coat beneath sets the background tone. Dark bases make silver and pewter vivid; warm bases deepen bronze and copper.

From design to finished floor

Design consultation. We review images you admire, discuss palette and movement and bring sample boards so you can judge the effect in person.

Preparation. The slab is ground flat and imperfections filled. Metallic resin reveals every low spot, so this stage is thorough.

Primer and base. These seal the concrete and establish the background tone.

The metallic pour. The pigmented resin is poured and worked across the entire room in one continuous session, creating the pattern.

Protection. A clear topcoat in gloss or satin adds resistance to scratches and, where needed, to sunlight.

Considerations before choosing metallic

Metallic floors reward the right setting. An honest summary:

AdvantagesPoints to plan for
AppearanceUnique, luminous, deepCannot be duplicated exactly
MaintenanceSeamless and simple to cleanGloss shows footprints sooner
DurabilityProtected by a clear topcoatGrit can dull a gloss finish
LightBeautiful in daylightUV-stable topcoat advised near windows
RepairsTopcoat may be renewedPattern repairs are difficult to blend

For a working garage or a mudroom, a flake floor is the more sensible choice. For a room where the floor is meant to be admired, metallic is difficult to better.

Climate and the metallic pour

Temperature has a strong influence on metallic work. In a cool room, resin thickens and pigment moves slowly; in a warm one, the working window shortens. Prescott homes experience both, especially in basements and seasonal rooms. We keep the space at a steady temperature before, during and after the pour.

Sunlight is the other consideration. At this elevation, UV through large windows can alter the tones of unprotected epoxy over time, so UV-stable topcoats are specified in bright rooms.

Why experience matters most here

Of all the systems we install, metallic is the least forgiving. The pour must be completed in one continuous session, mixing must be exact and technique must be practiced. Roller marks, visible seams between batches and dust in the surface cannot be corrected later. Before choosing an installer, ask to see sample boards and examples of completed floors.

Metallic floors in older homes

A metallic floor might seem an unusual choice for a period home, but in the right room it can work beautifully. Finished basements, rear additions and remodeled kitchens or family rooms often have a more contemporary character than the original house. Warm walnut and bronze palettes, finished in satin rather than high gloss, can bridge the gap between old and new.

Caring for a metallic floor

Dust mop regularly and damp mop with a pH-neutral cleaner. Felt pads beneath furniture and mats at exterior doors prevent most scratches. Avoid harsh chemicals and abrasive pads. In time, the topcoat can be lightly abraded and renewed to restore its clarity, leaving the decorative layer untouched.

How metallic floors are priced

Metallic floors sit at the upper end of our range because they involve additional layers, more material and a skilled, uninterrupted pour. Room size, the amount of flattening the slab requires, the number of pigments and the choice of topcoat all affect the final figure. Each project is priced after a visit.

Collector garages

Prescott is home to a good many classic and collector vehicles, and their owners often want a garage that does them justice. A metallic floor in pewter, slate or a deep bronze reflects the paint and chrome beautifully under good lighting and photographs well. For these spaces we specify a hard, chemical-resistant topcoat with a modest texture, and we recommend drip trays and tire mats beneath vehicles stored for long periods. The result is closer to a showroom than a garage, while remaining easy to keep clean.

Galleries and tasting rooms

In a gallery, a metallic floor can lend atmosphere without distracting from the work on the walls, provided the palette is restrained and the movement subtle. Tasting rooms and bars benefit from the same restraint, with a slightly tougher topcoat to cope with spills and the scuffing of stools. In both cases, we discuss lighting at the outset, since the floor's appearance depends heavily on how the room is lit.

What happens on the day of the pour

On the day the metallic layer goes down, the room is kept closed, warm and still. Doors and vents are managed to prevent drafts and dust, and the crew works the whole floor continuously from one side to the other. Once the pattern is set, the room is left undisturbed while the resin levels and cures. The household is asked to keep out of the space until we confirm it is ready for the topcoat, usually the following day.

Questions about metallic floors

Can you reproduce a floor I have seen elsewhere?

We can approach the palette and character closely and will show you sample boards first, but every metallic floor develops its own pattern and cannot be duplicated precisely.

Is a metallic floor easily scratched?

The clear topcoat protects the decorative layer. Gloss finishes show fine marks sooner than satin, so mats and furniture pads are recommended.

Would metallic suit my garage?

For a collector garage used mainly for display, yes. For a working garage, flake is more practical.

How long does the work take?

Allow several days from preparation to the final topcoat, plus curing time before furniture returns.

Is the surface slippery?

A gloss finish is smooth. In areas where slipping is a concern, a satin topcoat with fine texture can be used.

Can metallic be used in a gallery or restaurant?

Yes, with a tougher commercial topcoat. See our commercial flooring page.

Will it work over radiant heating?

Often, provided the heat is lowered during installation and raised gradually afterward. We confirm suitability beforehand.

Is metallic suitable for a busy commercial floor?

It can be, with a commercial-grade topcoat and good entrance matting. For very heavy traffic, a satin finish is more forgiving than gloss.

Can the pattern be directed in a certain way?

Yes, to a degree. We can encourage movement toward windows, along a hallway or around a focal point, though the final detail always forms naturally.

Can people stay in the house during the work?

In most cases, yes. We contain the work area and ventilate as needed, and we will advise if it would be more comfortable to be out for part of a day.
